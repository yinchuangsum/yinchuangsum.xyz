Last Thursday, about 40 of us packed into the AWS office at The Gardens, KL. No slides. No PowerPoint karaoke. Just laptops plugged into projectors, people hitting Enter, and the occasional "uh oh, it's rendering" silence.

The format was simple: Show & Tell. Raw demos, real failures, real wins. 11 demos across 4 hours. Someone called it "Malaysia's most honest AI meetup." Hard to argue.

Here's what I saw, grouped by thread.

## AI Coding & Tooling

**The 8-Hour Session**

One presenter kicked things off by showing his terminal. He'd been running an uninterrupted AI coding session for over 8 hours — Cursor + Claude chewing through roughly 200 sub-agent tasks in a single sitting. The output quality graph was telling: it tracked almost perfectly with how well he'd written the specs and documentation beforehand. Garbage in, garbage out still applies, even when the garbage compactor is an LLM.

**MachineIQ — 99% AI-Coded**

I presented next — MachineIQ, my capstone for the Gamuda AI Academy. The honest number: 99% of the code was generated by AI. Stack was OpenCode + Open Design + MCP skills (shoutout to Brewie). Running on two OpenCode Go plans — $20/month total.

My toolkit split: DeepSeek and Kimi for heavy coding, Claude and GPT for multi-modal work. The Chinese models are genuinely underrated in the Malaysian dev community. They're cost-efficient, they handle long contexts well, and they don't hallucinate as much as people assume.

**Portfolio Website Face-Off**

Someone in the audience ran the same prompt through Claude 5.6 Sonnet and GPT-4 for a portfolio site. Claude's output was noticeably better — cleaner layout, better typography, fewer "AI-generated" tells. Same prompt, same constraints, different league of output.

Also demoed an "I Have ADHD" productivity skill built for OpenCode. Because if the tools don't adapt to how your brain works, what's the point?

**Codebas — 3D in Two Prompts**

Codebas (from Women First Coding Community) live-demoed building an interactive 3D website in under an hour. Two prompts, Replit + Claude, Blender assets exported to WebGL. The whole thing went from idea to working URL in the time it takes most people to decide which framework to use.

## GenAI Video

This was the biggest energy spike of the night. Video is still the wild west, and everyone's hacking.

**Gabriel's $50 Experiment**

Gabriel spent $50 to resurrect a dead Facebook page with four AI anime videos. First problem: copyright filters ate everything. His workaround was brutal but effective — change character outfits until the original IP was unrecognizable. Messi became "some guy who plays soccer in a blue shirt." Key takeaway: AI video is a slot machine. The same prompt on different engines gives wildly different results. Try five approaches, not one.

**Batman Beyond, But Not**

Another founder with a film background tried making a Batman Beyond AI trailer using Hailuo Minimax. First attempt: blocked instantly — "Batman" is a copyrighted term. Second attempt: changed the prompt to "Terry McGinnis in a futuristic suit" — worked perfectly. The lesson: never use copyrighted names in your prompts. The model knows. The filters know. Be specific without being trademarked.

His pricing insight: for heavy video users, subscription pricing beats tokens every time. Token-based billing on video generation is a tax on experimentation.

**The General Workflow**

Someone walked through their Martini MCP + Cursor pipeline for AI video. The open secret: character consistency is not solved. Anyone claiming otherwise is selling something. Kling is currently the best rendering engine for quality, at roughly $3 for 15 seconds of 720p. Start and end frames are the real control mechanism — nailing those gives you reliable scene flow. Everything in the middle is negotiation.

## Hardware/Software Bridge

**NFC Lanyards at Career West**

Nathan from TalentBank demoed Career West — an AI job platform pairing CV uploads with career matching. The hardware hook: NFC-embedded lanyards. Tap someone's lanyard at an event and their CV lands on your phone instantly. Vietnam pilot is running at 600k VND per device. His diagnosis of the problem: "QR codes at networking events is damn troublesome." Can't argue with that.

**A Website You Can't Google**

The wildest thing I saw all night: a personal portfolio running on a microcontroller. To access it, you had to physically walk up to a hidden panel — like a secret bar entrance. Six thousand characters in the prompt, 90-95% worked first try. No URL. No SEO. Just a device in a wall. Completely useless for discoverability. Absolutely unforgettable as an experience.

## Data & Infrastructure

**Cerecity — Salary Transparency**

Cerecity is Malaysia's crowdsourced salary platform. ~3,000 downloads, all data verified. They built a 3D map visualization using MapRoulette + OpenStreetMap — dropping pins with salary data right on the map. If you've ever wondered whether you're being underpaid in Malaysia's tech scene, this is the dataset you want.

**Actrack — The 16-Year-Old Rails App**

HackerSpace @ MMU brought Actrack, a system that's replacing a Rails app from 2009. The stack: PostgreSQL + REST API + Discord bot. They're migrating from Heroku to Vercel. The presenter described the current state as "a three-sources-of-truth problem" — spreadsheet, the leader's brain, and the database, all claiming to be canonical. Painfully relatable to anyone who's worked in Malaysian tech.

**Gabriel's Local OCR**

Gabriel again, different project. A travel agency needed OCR — couldn't use cloud APIs because of client confidentiality. First attempt: web version using Fable. Failed. Second attempt: ByteDance's app-native OCR, built in 6 hours. Worked. His lesson: if a web wrapper keeps fighting you, go native. Sometimes the platform choice is the bottleneck, not the AI.

## Closing Themes

Walking out at 10pm, four themes stuck with me:

**Character consistency in GenAI video is not solved.** Anyone demoing AI video right now is showing you their best take, not their average take. The gap between those two is still a canyon.

**Chinese models are underrated for coding.** DeepSeek and Kimi deliver surprisingly good results at a fraction of the cost. The Malaysian dev community should be experimenting with them more.

**Spec quality determines output quality.** This is as close to a universal law as AI coding has right now. The time you spend writing a good spec is time you don't spend debugging bad generations.

**Subscriptions beat tokens for heavy users.** If you're generating video or running long coding sessions, the metering model punishes experimentation. Flat-rate pricing aligns incentives better.

**The Malaysian AI scene is skeptical, pragmatic, and open.** No gatekeeping, no hype cycles, no one trying to sell you a course. Just people showing what worked and what didn't. That's rare. That's worth protecting.
