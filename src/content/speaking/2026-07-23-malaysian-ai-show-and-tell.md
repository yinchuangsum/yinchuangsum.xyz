I presented MachineIQ, my capstone for the Gamuda AI Academy — 99% AI-coded using a stack of OpenCode, Open Design, and MCP skills (Brewie for requirement elicitation). The honest breakdown: two OpenCode Go plans at $20/mo total, with token routing to DeepSeek and Kimi for heavy coding, and Claude/GPT reserved for multi-modal and complex reasoning. The Chinese models are genuinely underrated in the local community — cost-efficient, good with long contexts, and less prone to hallucination than most assume.

The format is refreshingly direct: 40 people packed into the AWS office, 11 demos across 4 hours, laptops plugged into projectors, no slides. The energy spike of the night was the GenAI video segment — everyone hacking around copyright filters, fighting character consistency (not solved, anyone claiming otherwise is selling something), and figuring out whether subscription or token billing makes more sense for heavy usage. A founder with a film background showed how a "Batman" prompt gets blocked instantly but "Terry McGinnis in a futuristic suit" sails through — the model knows what you're doing.

Four themes that stuck: Chinese models are underrated for coding, spec quality directly determines output quality (this is as close to a universal law as AI coding has right now), subscriptions beat token billing for heavy users, and the Malaysian AI scene is skeptical, pragmatic, and open. No gatekeeping, no hype cycles, no one selling a course — just people showing what worked and what didn't. That's rare and worth protecting.
