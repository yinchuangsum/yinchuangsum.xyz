I was invited by **Wendy** to speak on a panel about balancing full-time work and startup validation. I shared my personal journey — from starting as a photographer after graduation (with zero income and deep financial insecurity) to self-teaching engineering in six months, joining an MNC, and working 9-to-5 plus side projects until midnight for 2–3 years before co-founding PigeonBook full-time.

The key advice I left the audience with: choose co-founders with complementary skills (my mistake was partnering with another engineer), treat impostor syndrome as a sign you're on the right track (I've hit rock bottom — everything else is up), and remember that networking is like speed dating — you'll get ignored, but the real work happens before the conversation by building reputation first.

Fellow panelist **Olivia** shared her experience building a university ice skating club from zero to 200 members and creating content for brands like Samsung and Pomelo. Wendy closed with a practical six-day validation framework: identify a frustrating problem, interview three people with the same pain, build a landing page, and get three strangers to pre-commit before building anything.

Grateful to Wendy for hosting and to the audience for the honest questions about mental resilience, co-founder selection, and impostor syndrome. These are the conversations that actually help people take the first step.
